# Pickling Brine Ratios

## Quick Pickles (Refrigerator Method)

For immediate use (ready in 24 hours, keeps 2–3 weeks), use 1:1 vinegar to water by volume. For a batch of 2 cups white or cider vinegar + 2 cups filtered water, add 3 tablespoons salt and 2 tablespoons sugar, bring to a boil, pour over sliced cucumbers or onions, and chill. The ratio is forgiving; taste after 12 hours and adjust sweetness or salt to preference.

## Shelf-Stable Canned Pickles

For heat-processed jars (requires proper canning knowledge), use 1.5:1 vinegar to water. For example, 3 cups 5% vinegar (store-bought or 1 part white vinegar diluted 1:1 with water) combined with 2 cups filtered water, plus 4 tablespoons salt and 1 tablespoon sugar per 2-quart batch. This higher acidity preserves safely. Pack hot jars and process in a water bath for 15 minutes (pints) or 20 minutes (quarts) at a rolling boil.

## Flavor Additions

Dill, garlic cloves, mustard seed, and black pepper are classic additions that do not affect shelf stability. Add fresh herbs after pickling for crisp texture or during brine-making for deeper flavor. For related preservation methods, see [[Preserved Lemons]] which use salt rather than vinegar. Also explore [[Yogurt Notes]] for other traditional preservation through fermentation.
