# Cast Iron Care Guide

## Seasoning the Pan

A bare cast iron skillet needs an initial season. Clean it thoroughly, dry completely, then coat thinly with a high-smoke-point oil (vegetable, canola, or avocado oil). Buff away excess oil with a cloth until almost no oil is visible. Bake at 450–500°F for 1 hour, then cool in the oven. Repeat this process 5–6 times to build a nonstick layer. Each bake adds a thin polymerized layer that improves durability and nonstick properties.

## Everyday Maintenance

After use, wash the pan with hot water and a stiff brush—avoid soap if the seasoning is light, or use minimal soap if necessary. Dry immediately with a cloth to prevent rust. For stuck-on food, use coarse salt and a bit of oil as a gentle abrasive; scrub the pan and wipe clean. After every use, apply a tiny dot of oil to the surface and buff it away, reinforcing the seasoning.

## Deep Seasoning and Restoration

Every few months, give the pan a refreshing seasoning: heat it gently on the stovetop, wipe with a thin coat of oil, then place in a 400°F oven for 20 minutes and cool. For a pan with rust spots or pitting, soak it overnight in white vinegar (vinegar dissolves rust but stops before damaging metal), scrub gently with a brush, dry thoroughly, and season immediately. Never put cast iron in the dishwasher or let it soak for extended periods.

## Storage and Prevention

Store cast iron in a dry place, never in a damp cabinet. If the kitchen is very humid, place a silica packet or paper towel inside the pan to absorb moisture. For multi-pan storage, lay them flat or hang them to prevent rust at contact points. A well-maintained pan should last generations and improve with age.
