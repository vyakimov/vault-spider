# Fridge Temperature Log

## Daily Monitoring Schedule

Record fridge and freezer temperatures every morning at 8 a.m. using the probe thermometer on the middle shelf (fridge) and in the center of a frozen water bottle (freezer). Fridge should hold 36–40°F; freezer should hold 0°F or lower. Log readings in a notebook kept on the fridge door for easy reference and to track seasonal variation.

## Acceptable Ranges and Alerts

Fridge temperature between 36–42°F is safe for food storage. If readings consistently climb above 42°F, the door seal may be weakening or the ambient room temperature is very warm. Freezer below 0°F preserves quality indefinitely; between 0 and 5°F is still safe but ice crystals form faster (freezer burn). Any reading above 5°F means the freezer is not safe and food should be consumed within days.

## Seasonal Variation

Summer months (June–August) show warmer readings by 2–3°F; this is normal if within the safe range. Winter readings can dip to 33–35°F without spoilage risk (actually beneficial for some items). If a heat wave drives readings dangerously high, avoid opening the fridge unnecessarily and consider temporarily moving perishables like [[Fermented Hot Sauce Notes]] to coolers with ice packs.

## Troubleshooting

If readings spike suddenly, check that the door closes fully and the seal is not dirty. Clean the gasket with a damp cloth and vinegar. If temps remain high after cleaning, call a repair technician. A 5°F swing is normal; gradual climbs over weeks signal a developing problem.
