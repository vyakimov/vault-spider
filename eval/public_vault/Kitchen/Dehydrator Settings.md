# Dehydrator Settings

## Herbs and Leafy Greens

Hang dry or spread herbs (basil, parsley, thyme) in a single layer. Set dehydrator to 95–105°F and run for 4–6 hours until completely crispy (no moisture when bent). Herbs are prone to scorching and losing volatile oils at high heat, so low-and-slow is key. Cool completely before storing in airtight jars away from light. Dried herbs last 6–12 months.

## Fruit Slices

Slice apples, strawberries, or other fruit 1/4-inch thick and arrange on trays without overlapping. Set to 135–150°F and run for 8–12 hours depending on fruit water content and thickness. Apples and firmer fruits take longer. Test by bending—fruit should flex slightly but not be sticky. Cool on the tray before storing.

## Vegetable Chips and Powders

Thinly slice (1/8-inch) tomatoes, beets, or zucchini. Set dehydrator to 120–135°F and run for 8–15 hours until completely dry and crispy. Once cool, grind into powder if desired (use a spice mill). Veggie powders concentrate flavor intensely—1 tablespoon of dried tomato powder equals several fresh tomatoes. Store in airtight containers with a bay leaf or oxygen absorber if long-term storage is planned.

## Energy Efficiency and Timing

Rotate trays every few hours if the dehydrator does not have a fan in the back, ensuring even drying. Herbs are fastest (4–6 hours), fruit medium (8–12 hours), and dense vegetables slowest (12–18 hours). Start checking at the low end of the range. Once dried, foods can be re-hydrated for cooking or stored dry indefinitely. Use dried herbs for spice blends like [[Spice Blend - House Curry]] and dried fruit and veggie chips as toppings for [[Pantry Bread]] toasts.
