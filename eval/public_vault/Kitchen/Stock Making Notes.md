# Stock Making Notes

## Clear Vegetable Stock

Cover vegetable scraps (onion skins, carrot peels, celery trimmings, parsley stems) with cold filtered water in a large pot. Bring to a simmer and immediately reduce heat to the lowest setting—the surface should barely bubble. Skim white foam from the top for the first 10 minutes. A clean, light stock comes from avoiding a hard boil. Simmer 45 minutes to 1 hour for vegetable stock; longer extraction muddies the flavor.

## Bone or Meat Stock

For deeper stock (beef or chicken bones), blanch bones in boiling water for 2 minutes, rinse thoroughly, then start with fresh cold water. Roast bones at 400°F for 30 minutes before simmering for additional color and depth. Simmer bones for 12–24 hours (chicken 8–12 hours, beef 20–24 hours) at the gentlest simmer; the liquid should barely move. Skim regularly, especially in the first hour.

## Straining and Skimming

Line a fine-mesh strainer with cheesecloth and pour stock slowly. Do not squeeze the solids or press them through; gentle straining yields a clear, clean-tasting stock. Cool to room temperature uncovered, then refrigerate. Fat will solidify on top and is easy to lift off once chilled. Store in the fridge (4 days max) or freeze in ice-cube trays for convenient portioning.

## Flavor Development

Add salt only at the end (stock reduces, concentrating salt). A bay leaf and 6–8 peppercorns complement most stocks without overpowering. For specific applications, pair with [[Preserved Lemons]] in poultry stock, or add a spice base from [[Spice Blend - House Curry]] for aromatic depth. Store stock in the cleanest containers, preferably ones that have been handled with care like cast iron—see [[Cast Iron Care Guide]] for handling principles. Always store stock according to [[Fridge Temperature Log]] guidelines. For shelf-stable stock, follow [[Pressure Canning Safety Notes]] if you wish to can it. Use stock generously in [[Pantry Bread]] gravy applications.
