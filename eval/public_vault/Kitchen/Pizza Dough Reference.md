# Pizza Dough Reference

## Dough Ratio and Hydration

A simple pizza dough uses 65% hydration for a crisp crust, 70–75% for a chewier, open crumb. For 500g all-purpose flour, mix 325g water (65%), 7.5g salt, and 2g instant yeast or 0.3g sourdough culture. A small amount of olive oil (10g) enriches the dough and improves browning. Knead for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic.

## Cold Fermentation

Skip the bulk ferment and do a cold overnight proof (8–14 hours in the fridge at 38–42°F) or a room-temperature bulk for 2–3 hours followed by overnight cold. Cold fermentation develops flavor and makes the dough easier to stretch (it relaxes the gluten). Divide into 100g balls for 10-inch personal pizzas or 200g for Sicilian-style thick pizzas.

## Shaping and Baking

Remove the dough ball from the fridge 30 minutes before baking to soften. Dimple the dough with your fingertips, working from the center outward, creating a shallow disk. Stretch gently by draping over your forearm; do not overwork. Top lightly and bake on a preheated pizza stone or steel at 500°F (or as hot as your oven goes) for 8–12 minutes. A properly hydrated dough bakes to a crisp, evenly browned crust with an open interior.

## Oven Spring and Color

Bake for 6–7 minutes on the stone before adding toppings if you prefer a drier base with toppings baked on. The dough will puff slightly during the first minute (oven spring) as yeast produces final gas. A high-heat bake (475–500°F) gives crust color in minutes; lower-heat ovens (400°F) require 15–18 minutes and produce less dramatic browning.
