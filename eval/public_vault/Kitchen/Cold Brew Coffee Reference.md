# Cold Brew Coffee Reference

## Ratio and Grind Size

Cold brew uses a coarse grind (like breadcrumbs or kosher salt texture) and a 1:4 coffee-to-water ratio by weight. For example, 100g coarse-ground coffee + 400g cold filtered water yields a concentrated cold brew. Use a French press, a mason jar with a fine mesh strainer, or a specialized cold brew maker. Combine ingredients in the vessel of choice, stir well to ensure all grounds are wet, and cover loosely (a cloth or paper towel prevents dust).

## Steep Time and Extraction

Steep at room temperature (68–72°F) for 12–24 hours. Longer steeps (18–24 hours) extract more fully and create a smoother, less acidic cup. Shorter steeps (12–16 hours) yield a brighter, more tea-like result. Temperature matters: if your kitchen is very warm, the steep completes faster; if cold, it may take the full 24 hours. Taste after 12 hours and decide if you want to continue steeping.

## Filtering and Dilution

After steeping, strain through a fine-mesh strainer lined with cheesecloth or a dedicated cold brew filter. Press gently on the grounds but do not squeeze (squeezing extracts bitter compounds). The resulting concentrate is thick and dark. Dilute 1 part concentrate with 1–2 parts cold water or milk to taste. Refrigerated concentrate keeps for up to 2 weeks; diluted cold brew keeps 4–5 days.

## Comparison to Other Methods

Cold brew differs from pour-over and other hot-brew methods in grind size (much coarser), water temperature (cold vs. hot), and brew time (12–24 hours vs. minutes). The extended cold steep produces a naturally smooth, low-acid concentrate ideal for iced coffee and mixed drinks.
