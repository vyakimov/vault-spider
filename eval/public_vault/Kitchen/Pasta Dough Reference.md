# Pasta Dough Reference

## Egg-to-Flour Ratio

The classic Italian ratio is 100g flour per egg (or roughly 3:1 flour to wet ingredients). For a family batch of 300g all-purpose flour, use 3 large eggs beaten together. Mound the flour on a clean work surface, create a well in the center, and pour in the eggs. Using a fork, gradually incorporate flour from the inner walls of the well until a shaggy dough forms. Work with your hands until the dough comes together into a rough ball.

## Kneading and Rest

Knead the dough for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic (it should not be sticky). If it's too wet, dust with more flour; if too dry, wet your hands slightly and continue working. Wrap in plastic wrap and rest at room temperature for 30 minutes. This resting period allows the gluten to relax and makes rolling easier.

## Rolling and Cutting

After resting, divide into quarters and work with one piece at a time (keep others covered). Using a pasta machine on the widest setting or a rolling pin, pass the dough through 5–6 times, folding in half between passes, until very thin (nearly translucent). Once thin enough, cut into desired shapes: fettuccine (1/4-inch), tagliatelle (3/8-inch), or fill for ravioli. Dry fresh pasta on a rack for 1–2 hours before cooking, or freeze for later.

## Cooking Time

Fresh pasta cooks in 2–4 minutes in salted boiling water, much faster than dried. Toss gently to prevent sticking. Test at 2 minutes; the pasta should have slight resistance when bitten (al dente).
