# Pressure Canning Safety Notes

## Low-Acid Food Processing

Low-acid foods (pH > 4.6) require pressure canning to kill *Clostridium botulinum* spores. Water-bath canning is unsafe for vegetables, meats, and soups. Use a pressure canner (not a pressure cooker, though some modern cookers have a canning function) and follow USDA or National Center for Home Food Preservation guidelines.

## Pressure and Time Standards

Dial gauge canners should be tested annually for accuracy; weighted gauge canners (15, 10, or 5 PSI models) are more reliable long-term. For altitude adjustments: at sea level to 1,000 feet, use 10 PSI; 1,001–2,000 feet use 11 PSI; above 2,000 feet add 1 PSI per 1,000 feet. Processing times vary by food: low-acid soups typically 75 minutes (quarts), vegetables 30–45 minutes (pints), meats 75–100 minutes (quarts). Never guess.

## Pre-Processing Checks

Inspect jars for cracks or chips; use only canning jars (not repurposed food jars). Fill jars leaving 1.25 inches headspace for most low-acid foods. Remove air bubbles by sliding a plastic spatula around the inside. Wipe rims clean so lids seal properly.

## During and After Processing

The pressure must reach and hold the correct PSI for the entire processing time. The moment you reach target pressure, start your timer. After processing, do not immediately vent the canner; let pressure return to zero naturally (20–30 minutes). Once cooled, remove jars and listen for the characteristic ping of lids sealing. Store unsealed jars in the fridge; sealed jars keep at room temperature for 1+ years.
