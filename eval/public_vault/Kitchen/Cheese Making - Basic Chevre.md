# Cheese Making - Basic Chevre

## Culture and Rennet

Start with one gallon of whole milk (not ultra-pasteurized). Heat to 86°F and add a liquid cheese culture (like Mesophilic or Creme Fraiche culture at 1/4 teaspoon per gallon) or a packet of direct-set chevre culture. Stir well for 1 minute, cover, and let sit undisturbed for 8–12 hours at room temperature (68–72°F). You should not need rennet for basic chevre; the culture alone curdles the milk.

## Draining and Straining

The milk will thicken into a custard-like mass. Line a colander with cheesecloth and carefully pour the curds and whey through, gathering the corners of the cloth. Suspend the bundle from a spoon or hook above a bowl and let it drip at room temperature for 6–8 hours, or overnight in the fridge for a drier, tangier cheese. The longer it drains, the firmer and more sour the final product.

## Finishing Touches

Once drained to your preferred consistency, transfer the curds to a bowl and blend with salt (about 1 teaspoon per gallon of starting milk) and fresh herbs (dill, chives, or tarragon) if desired. Taste and adjust seasoning. The finished chevre keeps refrigerated for up to 2 weeks in an airtight container. It spreads at room temperature, making it ideal for toast or crudités.
