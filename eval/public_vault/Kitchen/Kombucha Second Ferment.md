# Kombucha Second Ferment

## Flavoring Base

After the primary ferment (7–14 days, SCOBY removed), pour finished kombucha into clean bottles, reserving 10% (about 1 cup per quart of kombucha). Add flavorings: fruit juice (cranberry, ginger, or apple work well), whole fruit slices, or fresh herbs. A ratio of 1 part juice to 10 parts kombucha prevents excessive sweetness. Ginger and turmeric can be added as 1–2 teaspoons of fresh, chopped ginger per quart.

## Carbonation Timing

Cap bottles tightly and ferment 2–4 days at room temperature (68–72°F). The secondary ferment both carbonates the brew and consumes the remaining sugar. After 48 hours, open a bottle away from your face and listen for fizz. If you hear a hiss, it's ready to move to the fridge; if silent, let it continue another 24 hours.

## Carbonation Control

For highly carbonated kombucha (craft-soda level), use glass bottles with swing tops and ferment 3–4 days. For lightly carbonated (gentle bubbles), use 2–3 days. Too long and bottles can become dangerously pressurized. If a bottle hisses loudly when opened or sprays, it has been fermenting too long—chill it immediately and open carefully.

## Refrigeration and Storage

Once carbonated, move bottles to the fridge to stop fermentation. Secondary fermentation creates small sediment; pour carefully or strain. Refrigerated kombucha stays fizzy for 2–3 months.
