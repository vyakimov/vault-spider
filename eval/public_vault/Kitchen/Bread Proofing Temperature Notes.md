# Bread Proofing Temperature Notes

## Summer Proofing (72–78°F)

Bulk fermentation takes 3–4 hours, final proof 1–1.5 hours. At these warmer temperatures, watch the dough carefully; it can overproof if left unattended. A dough that has risen 50–75% and jiggles slightly when the bowl is tapped is ready for shaping. The final proof after shaping is 45–60 minutes depending on how warm the room is.

## Spring/Fall Proofing (65–70°F)

Bulk fermentation stretches to 4–5 hours, final proof 1.5–2 hours. This slower fermentation develops more flavor and gives a finer crumb. The dough should have good structure by the final proof, showing a slight dome when ready for the oven.

## Winter Proofing (55–62°F)

Cold kitchens require 6–8 hours bulk fermentation and 2–2.5 hours final proof. Many bakers in winter kitchens shift to cold overnight proofs (8–16 hours in the fridge at 38–42°F) to simplify timing. A cold final proof overnight produces excellent oven spring and color. Poke test: gently press your finger into the dough; it should spring back slowly (if very slowly, it's overproofed; if immediately, it needs more time).

## Temperature Adjustment Rule

For every 10°F drop in temperature, add roughly 50% to your proofing time. Conversely, every 10°F rise speeds things up by about 30%.
