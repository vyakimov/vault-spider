# Preserved Lemons

## Salting Process

Use unwaxed lemons if possible; scrub well and cut off the blossom end. Slice lemons lengthwise into quarters, leaving them attached at the stem end so they open like a flower. Work salt into the cut flesh: roughly 1.5 tablespoons salt per lemon. Pack them tightly into a clean, sterilized glass jar, pressing down as you layer. The lemon juice will be released by the salt and will gradually submerge the fruit.

## Curing Timeline

Leave at room temperature (60–75°F is ideal) for 2–4 weeks. The lemons will darken and the brine will become cloudy as natural fermentation begins. White or tan yeast on the surface is normal and protective; skim off any pink or black mold immediately. After 3–4 weeks, the rind will be soft and the bitter taste mellowed into a complex, umami-rich flavor.

## Using and Storing

Once cured, transfer to the fridge where they keep indefinitely (up to 1 year or longer). Use the flesh and rind in tagines, grain bowls, and braises—the salt is part of the seasoning, so adjust other salt in the recipe. A small piece of preserved lemon brightens stock or dressing.
