# Pest Log - Aphids

## 2024 Season Record

Early May: Light aphid presence on young transplants (peppers, eggplant). Managed through water spray (forceful spray dislodges aphids without pesticide application). Effectiveness: ~60% reduction in visible aphids after one week.

Mid-June: Heavier infestation on fennel and dill. Ladybug release (500 individuals, Coccinella septempunctata) introduced on 18 June. Population recovery observed within 3 days; aphid colonies largely suppressed by end of June.

Late July: Resurgence on undersides of kale and squash leaves. Insecticidal soap spray (5% potassium salts of fatty acids) applied on 25 July and 2 August. Two applications achieved 95% mortality within one week.

## 2025 Season Record

April–May: Preventive ladybug release (400 individuals) on 5 May coincided with peak vegetative growth. Aphid populations remained low (fewer than 10 per leaf during peak season). No corrective sprays required.

June–August: Sporadic light infestations quickly suppressed by resident ladybug populations. No intervention needed during these months.

## Control Summary

The community garden prioritizes biological control (ladybug releases) over chemical sprays. Ladybug releases cost approximately £25 per batch and are deployed in late spring (May) to establish populations before aphid peak season. Water spray serves as a first-line deterrent for light infestations. Insecticidal soap is reserved for situations where biological control fails or populations explode suddenly.
