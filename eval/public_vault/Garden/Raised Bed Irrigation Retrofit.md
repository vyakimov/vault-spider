# Raised Bed Irrigation Retrofit

## Background and Justification

The community garden's six raised beds (1.2 m × 2.4 m × 0.3 m deep) originally relied entirely on volunteer hand-watering with hoses. During the dry summer of 2023, plots received inconsistent watering (3–5 days between sessions), resulting in drought stress and reduced yields, particularly for water-demanding crops (squash, cucumber, tomato).

A drip irrigation retrofit was designed in early 2024 to address this inconsistency and reduce volunteer burden.

## System Design and Installation

Drip line (16 mm diameter, pressure-rated for 2 bar) runs along the bed length, positioned at soil surface below the mulch layer. Emitters are spaced 30 cm apart along the line, delivering 2 liters per hour per emitter. Six beds × 8 emitters per bed = 48 total emitters drawing approximately 96 liters per hour at full flow.

Supply is connected to the community's main hose bib via a 25 mm diameter hose. A battery-operated timer (two independent schedules) automates watering: 06:00 and 18:00 daily, each cycle running for 45 minutes. This delivers approximately 70 liters per bed per 24 hours during the growing season.

## Maintenance and Integration

Drip lines are flushed monthly to clear sediment from emitters. Filters on the supply hose are cleaned every six weeks. In October, all drip lines are drained and stored indoors to prevent freeze damage; reconnection occurs in May following spring bed preparation.

The retrofit works well in conjunction with row covers (used for brassica protection); drip lines are positioned to deliver water underneath covers without waterlogging foliage. See [[Pest Log - Cabbage Moths]] for notes on simultaneous management of row covers and automated irrigation.
