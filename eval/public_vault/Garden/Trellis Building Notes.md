# Trellis Building Notes

## Cattle Panel Construction

The community garden's primary trellis structures use 1.6 m × 2.4 m wire cattle panels (steel, approximately £35 each) mounted vertically between 7.5 cm wooden posts spaced 2.4 meters apart. Panels are secured with U-bolts and wire clips at the top and middle, allowing removal and repositioning without permanent installation.

Each panel supports 15–20 kg of mature bean or cucumber growth without sagging. The 10 cm × 10 cm grid opening is ideal for twining vines; plants establish rapidly and require minimal additional support.

## Alternative Trellis Forms

A-frame trellises (constructed from 2×2 lumber at a 60° angle) occupy 1.5 m² footprint and reach 1.8 m height. These are positioned on bed edges and support climbing crops (peas, scarlet runner beans) with minimal structural support once plants intertwine. A-frames can be disassembled and stored in the shed during winter.

Tripod forms (three bamboo poles lashed at the apex, 1.2 m height) are used for dwarf beans and young pea seedlings. These are lightweight, moveable, and suitable for plots where permanent structures are not desired.

## Vine Management and Seasonal Storage

As vines mature and become heavy, periodic tying and direction of growth prevents tangling and ensures balanced loading. Strings are monitored weekly; any tie cutting into a stem is loosened immediately.

At season end (October), spent plants are removed and composted. Trellis structures are inspected for damage, rust is light-sanded and oiled, and panels are stored vertically against the shed wall to prevent warping over winter.
