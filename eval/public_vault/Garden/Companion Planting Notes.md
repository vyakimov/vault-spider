# Companion Planting Notes

## Strong Pairings

Tomatoes benefit from basil planted at the perimeter of beds, with reported 15–20% pest reduction over two seasons. Squash planted with beans allows beans to climb naturally, though competition for water can occur during drought. Lettuce and spinach thrive in the shade cast by taller crops, extending the harvest window into summer months.

Carrots and onions planted in alternating rows appear to reduce carrot rust fly pressure. The community beds have maintained this pairing in the north section since 2023 with improved emergence rates.

## Problematic Combinations

Bean and garlic should not be placed adjacent, as garlic inhibits nitrogen fixation by rhizobia bacteria, negating one of the primary benefits of crop rotation. Cabbage and kale in the same raised bed elevate risk of cross-pollination if left to flower, and shared pest vulnerabilities (cabbage moths, flea beetles) make management difficult.

Cucumber vines should be spaced well away from aromatic herbs like dill; volatile compounds can reduce flowering and fruit set in adjacent plants.
