# Cold Frame Construction

## Frame Materials and Sizing

Cold frames for the garden's spring seedling program are built from rough-cut 2×8 boards (pressure-treated, 1.2 m wide, 0.9 m deep) with salvaged single-pane windows as lids. Each frame measures approximately 1.0 m × 0.8 m interior, with internal depth of 20 cm at the back sloping to 15 cm at the front to optimize sun angle.

Two frames are maintained permanently; a third is constructed in late February each year and dismantled after the June transplant season.

## Installation and Site Selection

Frames are positioned on a south-facing slope to maximize early-morning and afternoon sun exposure. Soil beneath each frame is enhanced with 10 cm of finished compost to create a warm root zone. The ground should slope slightly for water drainage; pooling indicates poor site selection.

Frames are oriented with the sloped face angled southward (15–20° inclination is ideal). Edge boards are leveled carefully; uneven frames trap water and cause seedling damping-off.

## Ventilation and Watering

On sunny days above 15°C, frames must be propped open to prevent overheating. A simple wooden wedge (5 cm) placed under the window frame allows airflow without full opening. Closing at dusk retains overnight warmth. Automatic vent openers (bi-metallic or liquid-filled) are not used in this garden; manual management provides better oversight.

Water only in early morning. Seedlings in frames are vulnerable to fungal diseases; evening watering promotes conditions that favor mold and rot.
