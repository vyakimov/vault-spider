# Garden Tool Maintenance

## Cleaning and Derusting

In early October, all community garden hand tools (spades, forks, hoes, rakes) are brought to the shed for seasonal maintenance. Soil and organic matter are scrubbed away with a wire brush. Tools showing surface rust are treated by rubbing with steel wool and light oil; heavier rust is addressed by wire-brushing or brief immersion in rust-removal solution (vinegar and water, 1:1).

The process takes approximately 4 hours for the 35-tool inventory. Tools are cleaned in batches of 8–10 to allow inspection of handles for damage.

## Sharpening

Cutting edges (spade, hoe, edger) should be sharpened twice yearly—mid-season and at the end of growing season. A mill bastard file at a 25–30° angle produces an effective working edge for garden soil. Pruners are sharpened quarterly, with old pruners donated once beyond reasonable restoration.

Dull tools require 30% more effort to use and increase fatigue among volunteers; sharp tools are a safety priority.

## Oiling and Storage

After cleaning and sharpening, all metal surfaces are lightly oiled with linseed oil or machine oil to prevent rust formation over winter. Handles (wood or plastic) are inspected for cracks; any tool with a compromised handle is removed from service. Repaired tools are returned to inventory; irreparable tools are recycled.

See [[Garden Shed Inventory]] for the current list. Winter storage in the shed should follow a set layout to prevent damage.
