# Rainwater Barrel Maintenance

## Cleaning and Inspection

Barrels should be emptied and scrubbed annually in late autumn before winter dormancy. Use a soft brush and water rinse; avoid high-pressure washers that can damage internal walls and fittings. Inspect for cracks, corrosion around the spigot, and sediment accumulation (typically 2–3 cm of fine silt at the bottom).

Four barrels serve the community beds, each with a 200-liter capacity. Maintenance is rotated among volunteers on a quarterly schedule to distribute effort.

## First-Flush Diverter Maintenance

The first-flush diverter on each barrel diverts the initial 25–50 liters of roof runoff (which contains leaves, bird droppings, and dust) away from storage. Check the diverter valve monthly; clear accumulated debris from the external screen.

After heavy rain events, manually flush the diverter by opening the bypass valve to confirm flow returns to the barrel. Leaves should not obstruct the screen; keep downspout guards clear.

## Winterization

In regions with sustained sub-zero temperatures, barrels must be drained completely before the first frost to prevent ice damage to fittings. Spigots should be removed and stored indoors. Covers should remain in place to prevent debris accumulation and algal growth during dormancy.

Refill and reconnect systems in early spring once ground thaw is complete. Test all connections for leaks before relying on barrels for irrigation.
