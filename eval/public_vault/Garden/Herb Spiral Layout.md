# Herb Spiral Layout

## Spiral Structure and Hydrology

The community garden's herb spiral is constructed from stacked stone rising 90 cm at the apex, forming a spiral base with a footprint of 1.8 m diameter. The structure creates a water gradient: rainwater entering at the top drains downward through the spiral, accumulating moisture at the base and remaining drier at the apex.

This natural hydrologic pattern allows a single 2 m² structure to accommodate herb species with vastly different moisture requirements without separate irrigation or amendment. The spiral was completed in spring 2024 and plantings were established over May–June.

## Plant Placement and Rationale

Rosemary occupies the uppermost apex position (25–30 cm height) where drainage is most rapid and soil remains driest. Rosemary prefers a dry root zone and flowers well in lean, well-drained soil. Thyme and oregano are positioned in the middle sections (40–50 cm height), tolerating moderate moisture.

Mint is contained in a 30 cm deep pot embedded at the spiral base, where the soil is damp. This containment prevents invasive rhizome spread while providing the moisture mint prefers. Mint plants derive from divisions and are replaced every 2–3 years.

## Maintenance

The spiral is watered at the top; water infiltrates naturally and supplies moisture to all layers. No supplemental watering of the base is needed except during extreme drought. Annual mulching with fine gravel (at the dry apex sections) and leaf debris (at the damp base sections) maintains the layered structure.
