# Perennial Bed Rotation

## Static Herb Positions

The perennial herb bed (east fence line, 4.5 m × 1.2 m) contains rosemary, lavender, thyme, and oregano planted in 2023. Unlike annual vegetable beds, these plants remain in place year to year. Soil here is amended every spring with 5 cm of compost, but the plants themselves do not move.

Perennial rootstocks develop deep anchorage and benefit from continuity. Uprooting and replanting annually causes stress, reduced flowering, and increased mortality. The herb bed thus represents a permanent fixture in garden planning.

## What Does Rotate

Mint is confined to a buried pot (30 cm deep) to prevent invasive rhizome spread. This pot is removed in late autumn for cleaning and storage, then reinstalled in spring—the only form of mint "rotation." Without this protocol, mint colonizes adjacent beds within one season.

Asparagus and rhubarb occupy their own dedicated zones (1.5 m² each) and are never moved. The rhubarb crown receives fresh compost annually in spring.

## Trellis Integration

Climbing herbs (hops, scarlet runner beans) grown on the perennial bed perimeter can rotate between supports. See [[Trellis Building Notes]] for details on constructing lightweight panels that allow seasonal adjustment of the climbing network without disturbing herb plantings.
