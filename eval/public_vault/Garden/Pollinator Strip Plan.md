# Pollinator Strip Plan

## Layout and Spacing

The 20-meter pollinator strip runs along the south boundary of the community garden. Planting is staggered in two rows, with flowering plants spaced 30–40 cm apart to allow mature canopy overlap without denseness that promotes fungal disease.

The strip receives 6–8 hours direct sunlight daily and is positioned away from herbicide drift from nearby properties. Soil was amended with 8 cm of aged compost in spring 2025.

## Bloom Succession Planning

Early season (April–May): creeping phlox and hellebores provide initial forage.

Mid-season (June–August): coneflowers, black-eyed Susans, and salvias sustain bees through the hottest weeks.

Late season (September–October): asters and solidago support monarch migration.

Continuous bloom is the goal; there should be flowering somewhere in the strip at all times during the growing season.

## Maintenance and Mulch

The strip is edged with boards and mulched to reduce water competition with adjacent lawn. Refer to [[Mulch Types Comparison]] for mulch selection and application rates. Dead-heading is performed weekly during peak bloom to extend flowering. Spent flower heads are left standing in November to provide overwintering habitat for beneficial insects.

Do not fertilize the strip; native species and adapted perennials perform better in lean soil and produce more nectar when not oversupplied with nitrogen.
