# Soil pH Testing Guide

## pH Meter Operation and Calibration

The community garden maintains a shared Hanna Instruments HI98103 soil pH meter for volunteer use. The meter must be calibrated before each testing session using two buffer solutions (pH 4.0 and 7.0). Calibration takes 3 minutes and ensures readings are accurate to ±0.1 pH units.

To test soil pH: collect soil samples from 5–10 cm depth, moisten with distilled water to a muddy consistency, insert the probe, allow 30 seconds for reading stabilization, and record the value. Never press the probe into soil; simply rest it gently in the sample.

## Recalibration Schedule

Buffers are replaced annually (typically in January after winter storage). The meter's battery is checked twice yearly; a dead battery produces erratic readings. Electrode maintenance (rinse with distilled water after each use, never tap-clean the probe) extends the meter's useful life to 4–5 years.

Volunteer coordinators receive a brief training session on proper handling; the meter is stored in its case with buffer solutions in a cool corner of the shed.

## Interpretation and Action

Most vegetable crops prefer pH 6.0–7.0. Community beds typically range 6.2–6.8, requiring no amendment. If any bed tests below 5.5 (acidic), lime is applied at a rate of 100–150 grams per square meter; above 7.5 (alkaline), sulfur amendment at 30–50 grams per square meter is considered.
