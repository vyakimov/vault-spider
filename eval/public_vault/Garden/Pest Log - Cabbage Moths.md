# Pest Log - Cabbage Moths

## 2023 Season

The community garden experienced high pressure from cabbage white moths (Pieris rapae) throughout July–August. Caterpillar infestations destroyed approximately 40% of brassica crops (cabbage, broccoli, kale). Damage was concentrated on beds without protection, while one plot shielded with lightweight row cover suffered only 5% damage.

This observation prompted adoption of row cover as the primary control strategy for the 2024 season.

## 2024 Season Response

White row cover (30% UV transmission, 0.45 oz/yd²) was deployed over all brassica beds immediately after transplanting. The physical barrier prevents adult moths from accessing plants; no caterpillars developed in covered beds. Covers were lifted weekly for inspection and removed entirely when plants reached flowering stage (approximately 60 days post-transplant).

The covers remained in place for 8 weeks per bed, corresponding to two complete cabbage moth life cycles. Damage to covered beds: 0%. Covered beds yielded approximately 30% higher harvest than uncovered control beds.

## Integrated Management

Row cover is the primary defense. For late-season or uncovered plantings, hand-removal of visible egg clusters (yellow masses on leaf undersides) prevents future infestations. See [[Raised Bed Irrigation Retrofit]] for related field notes on managing irrigation around row covers. Detailed cultivation notes for brassicas appear in the crop guidance documents updated seasonally by the volunteer coordinator.

Cost of row cover material: approximately £40 per season for the full community garden. Labor to deploy and store covers: 4 hours annually.
