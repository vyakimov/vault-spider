# Fruit Tree Pruning Calendar

## Apple Trees

The garden maintains two espalier-trained apple trees (Freedom variety) trained horizontally along the north fence. Dormant-season pruning occurs in late February, when buds are swelling but before any leafing occurs. This timing allows the tree to compartmentalize wounds and minimize disease infection.

Pruning reduces branch growth to enforce the horizontal espalier form and removes any crossing or inward-facing growth. Approximately 30–40% of the previous season's shoot growth is removed annually. This aggressive pruning maintains the flat canopy while promoting fruiting.

## Pear Trees

A single Beurre Bosc pear tree (center fence position, free-standing form) receives lighter dormancy pruning in late February. Remove only dead or diseased wood and crossing branches; pears form fruit on two-year-old wood, so heavy pruning reduces yields.

A secondary summer pruning (early August) removes excess vegetative growth that shades developing fruit and restricts air circulation. This light cut improves ripening and pest monitoring.

## Timing and Wound Healing

All pruning is completed by late February to allow trees to leaf out and seal wounds during the active growing season. Cuts larger than 2.5 cm diameter are left unwound (wound dressing is no longer recommended). Small branches heal completely within one growing season; larger branches may take 2–3 years.

Never prune in autumn; the tree's energy is directed to root storage rather than wound compartmentalization, increasing disease risk.
