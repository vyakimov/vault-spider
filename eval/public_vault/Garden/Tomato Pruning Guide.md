# Tomato Pruning Guide

## Sucker Removal

Indeterminate varieties planted in the community beds should have suckers removed weekly once plants reach 30 cm height. Suckers form at the angle between the main stem and lateral branches; removing them directs energy into fruit production rather than foliage. Use sharp pruners and wipe between cuts to avoid spreading disease.

The primary leader should retain all true leaves. Secondary sucker removal typically continues until mid-August, when stopping promotes ripening of remaining fruit before frost.

## Topping and Flowering

Four to six weeks before the predicted first frost, top plants by removing the terminal growing point. This prevents the plant from allocating resources to flowers that will not mature. Each community garden plot should complete topping by early September for the standard regional frost date of 30 September.

## Pest and Disease Considerations

Late blight and early blight thrive in dense foliage with poor air circulation. Aggressive pruning of lower foliage (removing all leaves within 20 cm of the soil line) reduces humidity around the stem base and infection risk. See [[Pest Log - Cabbage Moths]] for integrated pest notes across the beds. Dense plantings may require extra vigilance; refer to [[Raised Bed Notes]] for spacing guidelines.
