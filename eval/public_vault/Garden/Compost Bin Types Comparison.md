# Compost Bin Types Comparison

## Wire Bin System

A single 1.2 m diameter wire enclosure, 1.2 m high, holds approximately 1.5 cubic meters of material. Cost is low (approximately £30 for galvanized wire and stakes). The wire design allows excellent airflow and easy fork access from all sides, making turning labour-intensive but efficient.

A wire bin reaches finished compost (dark, crumbly, earth-smell) in 8–12 weeks if actively turned weekly. The open design allows visual monitoring of decomposition progress. However, rodents can access material, and the bin becomes muddy during heavy rain.

## Tumbler System

A rotary tumbler (900 liter capacity, hinged access door) sits on a stand 60 cm above ground. Material is mixed by rotating the drum 5–10 times weekly. Tumblers are more expensive (£200–400) but require dramatically less physical effort than fork-turning. Finished compost is ready in 6–8 weeks.

Tumblers are rodent-proof and allow one-person operation. They are unsuitable for very wet material and produce smaller batches than static bins; the community garden maintains one tumbler for quick small-batch composting.

## Three-Bay Static System

Three wooden bays (each 1.2 m × 1.2 m × 1.0 m high) arranged in a row allow staged decomposition. Fresh material is added to bay one; after 3–4 weeks it is turned (forked) into bay two; after another 3 weeks it moves to bay three for final curing. This system manages large volumes (4+ cubic meters active at any time) and produces finished compost continuously.

The three-bay system requires significant labor for turning (45 minutes every 3 weeks for all three bays). See [[Herb Spiral Layout]] for detailed bay construction notes used in this garden. The community maintains this primary system, with one dedicated volunteer coordinator managing the turning schedule.
