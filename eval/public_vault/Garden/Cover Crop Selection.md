# Cover Crop Selection

## Winter Rye

Cereal rye (sown late September, harvested early May) is a reliable nitrogen-scavenging cover crop that captures residual nutrients from summer gardening and prevents leaching over winter. Rye grows to 60–90 cm height and is tilled under 2–3 weeks before spring planting to allow decomposition.

Rye produces allelopathic compounds that persist briefly after incorporation; a 14-day wait between tilling and planting is standard practice. The crop yields approximately 3–4 tons per hectare of green biomass, adding significant organic matter to depleted beds. Approximately 150 grams of seed per 10 square meters is required.

## Red Clover

Red clover (Trifolium pratense) is a nitrogen-fixing cover crop sown in late August or early spring. Its rhizobia-bacterial association fixes atmospheric nitrogen, improving bed fertility without purchased input. Clover flowers and provides late-season forage for bees (if beds are designated for this use).

Clover does not grow as tall as rye (40–50 cm) and is easier to turn under. However, it requires more moisture during establishment and does not suppress weeds as effectively as rye. The decision between rye and clover depends on whether the garden prioritizes biomass accumulation (rye) or fertility and pollinator support (clover).

## Rotation Strategy

Most community beds use winter rye on a two-year rotation: one bed rests under rye while another produces vegetables. Beds are switched annually. Some smaller beds experiment with clover underseeding (clover interplanted with perennial herbs in late summer); this maintains perennial plantings while capturing nitrogen.
