# Compost Turning Schedule

## Three-Bay System Management

The community garden's compost system consists of three bays (each 1.2 m × 1.2 m × 1.0 m high). Fresh material enters Bay 1; material is turned from Bay 1 to Bay 2 every three weeks, then from Bay 2 to Bay 3 after another three weeks, for a total decomposition period of approximately 8–9 weeks.

Each turning takes approximately 45 minutes for a volunteer team of two people working with garden forks. Material is lifted, shaken to break compacted clumps, and restacked in the new bay.

## Weekly Contribution and Monitoring

All kitchen waste (vegetable scraps, eggshells, shredded paper) and garden waste (weeds, plant trimmings, grass clippings) are added to Bay 1 throughout the week. Material is alternated in layers: approximately 10 cm garden waste, then 5 cm kitchen scraps, then 10 cm garden waste again, to maintain a carbon-to-nitrogen ratio of roughly 25:1.

Every Monday, the Bay 1 manager inspects the pile for moisture (should be damp but not waterlogged), temperature (interior should be hot to touch if active decomposition is occurring), and odor (earthy smell indicates normal decomposition; ammonia or sulfur smells indicate problems requiring adjustments).

## Turning Schedule

- Weeks 1–3: Material accumulates in Bay 1
- Week 3 Sunday: Turn Bay 1 → Bay 2
- Weeks 4–6: Bay 1 receives fresh material; Bay 2 heats and breaks down
- Week 6 Sunday: Turn Bay 2 → Bay 3
- Weeks 7–9: Bay 3 undergoes final curing
- Week 9 Sunday: Finished compost from Bay 3 is sifted and distributed to community beds

See [[Perennial Bed Rotation]] for notes on how finished compost is distributed across rotational beds. Detailed troubleshooting for cold or wet piles is available in existing garden documentation.
