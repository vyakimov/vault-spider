# Slug and Snail Deterrents

## Copper Tape Method

Copper tape (50 mm width, 0.05 mm thickness) applied around raised bed edges deters slugs and snails through galvanic reaction when the mollusks' slime layer contacts copper. The tape is most effective within the first 4 weeks of installation; as weathering dulls the copper surface, efficacy declines.

Copper tape costs approximately £0.80 per meter. For four community beds (average 8 meters perimeter each), annual expenditure is around £25. Efficacy is reduced during wet seasons when high humidity increases slime production.

## Beer Traps

Half-filled glasses or cans of stale beer (3 cm depth) set at soil level attract slugs and snails, which drown. Traps should be emptied and refilled every 3–4 days. This method works well during dry periods but becomes ineffective during heavy rain, when slugs disperse across the garden rather than congregating at single points.

Beer traps are labor-intensive but require no purchased materials beyond beer. Community volunteers report 60–80 slugs caught per trap per week during peak season (May–July).

## Evening Handpicking

Direct collection during dusk and dawn (when slugs are most active) is effective for smaller beds. Gloved volunteers walk bed edges with a bucket, removing visible snails and slugs. This method requires discipline but integrates pest monitoring into routine garden work.

Over the 2024 season, evening handpicking reduced damage to seedlings by approximately 40% compared to untreated beds. Combined with copper tape on high-value plantings, this two-tier approach balances labor and material costs.
