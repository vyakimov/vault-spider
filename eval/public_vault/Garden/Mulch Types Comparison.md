# Mulch Types Comparison

## Straw

Wheat straw at 10 cm depth suppresses weeds effectively when applied over cardboard base layers. Moisture retention is good, with mulched beds retaining 20–25% more soil water during drought than unmulched plots. However, straw introduces seed—oat varieties and volunteer grass seedlings emerge occasionally. Cost is approximately £2 per bale for roughly 20 m² coverage.

Straw deteriorates over one season, requiring annual reapplication. Labor cost for spreading and removing spent mulch can exceed material cost in smaller plots.

## Wood Chip

Coarse hardwood chips (2–4 cm pieces) break down slowly, lasting 18–24 months. Weed suppression is excellent at 8–10 cm depth. Fresh wood chips can temporarily immobilize nitrogen as microorganisms decompose cellulose, so application over soil rather than over existing compost or high-nitrogen amendments is recommended.

Wood chips are excellent around perennial herb beds and shrubs. Avoid fresh pine chips in vegetable areas.

## Leaf Mulch

Finely shredded leaves decompose within 6–9 months, adding organic matter directly to topsoil. This is the preferred mulch for annual beds. Leaves are collected from community grounds and shredded with a garden shredder, resulting in near-zero material cost.

Leaf mulch requires seasonal renewal but eliminates purchased inputs. Fall application allows winter decomposition, so beds are ready for spring planting without additional tillage.
