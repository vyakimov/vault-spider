# Seed Saving Basics

## Easy Crops for Community Gardens

Beans (snap and dry varieties) mature and dry on the plant; pods are harvested when brown and brittle, then threshed by hand. Isolation distance is minimal (1.5 meters between varieties) due to the flower structure. Two seasons of community saving have produced viable seed lots with 85–90% germination.

Lettuce bolts in late summer, flowering reliably without intervention. Seeds are collected by allowing flower stalks to dry completely, then stripping seed heads into a paper bag. Lettuce sets seed within the same plot cycle with minimal isolation requirements.

Peas (shelling and snow types) are kept separate but require only row distance (1 meter) to prevent cross-pollination. Dry pods are collected, shelled, and stored in paper envelopes.

## Isolation and Spacing Distances

Squash and melon varieties must be separated by at least 400 meters to prevent cross-pollination. Community gardens cannot reliably save these; purchased seed is recommended annually.

Tomatoes are self-fertile and require only 2–3 meters distance between varieties to avoid mechanical mixing during harvest. Fermentation of seeds in water for two days before drying improves germination.

## Storage and Viability

Saved seeds are dried in the garden shed to below 8% moisture content before storage. They are kept in paper envelopes (never plastic, which traps moisture) in cool, dry conditions. Most garden seeds remain viable for 3–5 years. Refer to [[Cold Frame Construction]] for hardening off seedlings started from saved stock.
