# Mercury Transit Prediction Notes

## Upcoming Transit Events

Mercury transits the solar disk on predictable schedules: 13 May 2026 (afternoon visibility, 7.6 hour duration), 7 November 2026 (morning visibility, 5.3 hour duration). Both transits are visible from northern hemisphere locations; optimal viewing requires solar filters and specialized imaging equipment.

## Observational Opportunities

The May 2026 transit offers the best terrestrial viewing due to high solar altitude at transit midpoint (40+ degrees), minimizing atmospheric distortion. Second contact ingress at 13:05 UTC; third contact egress at 20:52 UTC. Weather permitting, continuous imaging through the entire event enables precise timing measurements and limb profile analysis.

## Reference Data for Planning

Transit entry velocity approximately 12.7 arcseconds/minute at first contact, decelerating to 8.3 arcseconds/minute near mid-transit due to projection geometry. Angular diameter on the solar disk: 9.8 arcseconds. Shadow bands and Baily's beads effects are not visible during Mercury transits due to the planet's small angular diameter.
