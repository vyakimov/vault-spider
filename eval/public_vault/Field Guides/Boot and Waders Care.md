# Boot and Waders Care

## Why Cleaning Matters

Invasive seeds (e.g., curly dock, Japanese knotweed) and aquatic pathogens (e.g., zebra mussel veligers) easily cling to boot treads and neoprene wader material. Moving contaminated equipment between the North Ridge and South Meadow stations can inadvertently transport pathogens across the watershed. All footwear and waders must be scrubbed between site visits.

## Cleaning Procedure

1. Use the boot scraper at each station entrance to remove caked mud and plant matter.
2. Submerge boots in the rinse bucket (kept by the equipment cabinet) and agitate for 30 seconds.
3. Use a stiff-bristled brush on boot treads, paying special attention to gaps and edges.
4. Drain and air-dry boots upright for at least 2 hours before use at another site.
5. For waders, rinse the exterior with fresh water and hang vertically in shade; do not fold or wring.

## Drying Between Seasons

After the final field visit of the season (October for most of the team), rinse waders thoroughly and hang them in a well-ventilated space indoors. Store dry by hanging on a wide hanger—avoid crease creases, which can crack the material over time. Do not store waders in direct sunlight, which degrades neoprene.

## Equipment Inspection

Before spring season (late March), inspect all boots and waders for cracks, separation at seams, or material degradation. Waders with small punctures can be patched with a neoprene repair kit. Boots with sole separation should be replaced.
