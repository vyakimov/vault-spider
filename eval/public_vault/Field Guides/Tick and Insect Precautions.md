# Tick and Insect Precautions

## Repellent and Protective Clothing

During warm-season field visits (May through September), wear long sleeves and long trousers tucked into boot tops when working in tall grass or brush. Clothing should be light-coloured to make attached ticks visible.

Apply DEET-based insect repellent (10% concentration minimum) to all exposed skin and the cuffs of sleeves and trousers 15 minutes before entering the field. Reapply after 4 hours of continuous exposure or after sweating.

An alternative is permethrin-treated clothing; spray or soak fieldwork garments in permethrin solution (0.5%) and allow to dry fully before wearing. Permethrin kills ticks on contact and persists through multiple launderings.

## Post-Visit Tick Check

Return to the vehicle immediately after fieldwork is complete and conduct a thorough self-check:

- Remove all clothing and inspect seams, waistbands, and sock cuffs (ticks prefer these areas)
- Run hands over the entire body, feeling for bumps or unusual texture
- Pay particular attention to armpits, groin, behind ears, and hairline
- If a tick is found attached (engorged or partially engorged), remove it with tweezers by grasping as close to the skin as possible and pulling straight outward; do not twist or crush
- Place the tick in a sealed plastic bag and discard it

## Laundry and Equipment Care

After each field day, wash clothing in hot water and dry on high heat for at least 10 minutes to kill any unseen tick nymphs. Do not mix potentially contaminated field clothes with other laundry until they have been heat-treated.

Store field boots and waders in a separate area; ticks can persist for weeks in dark, humid conditions.
