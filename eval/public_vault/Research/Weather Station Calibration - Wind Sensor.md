# Weather Station Calibration - Wind Sensor

## Anemometer Calibration Approach

The three-cup anemometer is calibrated quarterly (every 3 months) using the direct rotation method. The cups are manually spun at a known angular velocity (counted rotations per minute), with the data logger output compared to the expected pulse frequency. A hand-held tachometer provides independent reference measurement at speeds ranging from 0 to 300 rpm.

## Wind Direction Sensor Verification

The wind vane is checked monthly for mechanical stiction by gently moving the vane through 360 degrees. Free rotation without binding is required; any friction indicates bearing contamination requiring in-situ lubrication. Magnetic declination is verified annually (currently 12.5 degrees east) to ensure north-zero calibration remains accurate for true north reporting.

## In-Situ Calibration Methodology

Every second year, wind speed is cross-checked against a portable cup anemometer placed at identical height. A calm-wind period is used to verify zero offset—any offset > 0.2 m/s triggers replacement of the Hall effect sensor. Vibration and metallic noise (static discharge from lightning) are assessed audibly during calm conditions; damaged cup surfaces are replaced if pitting exceeds 2 mm in any area.

## Seasonal Considerations

Calibration is scheduled before and after high-wind seasons (winter and spring) to detect wear patterns. Cup replacement threshold is set at 100,000 cumulative rotations, typically reached every 3–4 years given average site wind speeds of 4–6 m/s.
