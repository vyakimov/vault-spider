# Pollinator Transect Protocol

## Transect Layout and Distance

The pollinator transect follows a 500 m route through mixed shrubland and riparian woodland, marked with GPS waypoints at 50 m intervals. The route remains fixed to enable temporal comparisons across seasons and years. The observer walks the transect at a constant pace of 1 km/hr, covering the full distance in 30 minutes.

## Visual Detection and Recording

All insects landing on flowers within a 2 m vertical band (1 m on each side of the transect line) are recorded. Observations include insect morphotype (bee, wasp, fly, lepidopteran, or other), flower species visited, and time of arrival and departure. Identification to species is attempted for native bees; non-native species are grouped by genus. Camera distances range from 1–3 m, requiring careful approach technique to avoid startling insects.

## Seasonal and Diurnal Timing

Transect walks are conducted during warm, sunny conditions (air temperature above 18°C, solar radiation above 600 W/m²) on fixed dates in May, July, and September. Morning sessions start at 10:00, while afternoon sessions start at 14:00, allowing documentation of diurnal activity shifts. Weather variables are recorded at transect start and end, including cloud cover, wind speed (Beaufort scale), and temperature.
