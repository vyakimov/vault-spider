# Invasive Species Monitoring Log

## Target Species and Detection Methods

Three invasive species receive focused monitoring: spotted knapweed, Japanese knotweed, and cheatgrass. Monthly walk-throughs of the riparian zone document new infestations and track removal efforts. Detection uses visual surveys along established transects, with GPS waypoints recorded for new patches exceeding 1 m² coverage.

## Removal Efforts and Timing

Spotted knapweed is hand-pulled in June before flowering, with root extraction to 10 cm depth when soil moisture permits. Japanese knotweed is cut monthly during the growing season (June–September), with cut stems treated with herbicide (glyphosate 5% aqueous solution) on the same day to prevent regrowth. Cheatgrass removal is impractical at landscape scale; monitoring focuses on preventing spread into core native plant zones.

## Long-Term Trend Tracking

Knapweed density has decreased from 2 plants/m² (2020) to 0.3 plants/m² (2025) following four years of systematic removal. Japanese knotweed patches remain relatively stable at 3–5 discrete patches; complete eradication requires 5+ years of continuous treatment. Annual monitoring in late July documents seed production timing and informs removal scheduling for the following season.
