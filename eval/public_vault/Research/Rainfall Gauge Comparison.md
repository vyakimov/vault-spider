# Rainfall Gauge Comparison

## Gauge Type Selection and Trade-Offs

Two rainfall gauge designs were evaluated in parallel for the weather station: a tipping-bucket mechanism and a weighing lysimeter gauge. The tipping-bucket offers simplicity and digital resolution (0.01 inch per tip), while the weighing gauge provides continuous measurement and captures rainfall rate profiles. Testing through the 2025 wet season quantified systematic differences in both accuracy and data utility.

## Tipping-Bucket Testing Results

The tipping-bucket gauge logs 148 tips over a 25 mm rainfall event, corresponding to 18.80 mm measured by a slant-plate comparator. Wind-induced oscillations were observed during events with gusts exceeding 8 m/s, resulting in phantom tips of ~0.5 mm per high-wind period. The 0.01 inch threshold (0.254 mm) favors detection of light drizzle but can miss gradational transitions in rainfall intensity.

## Weighing Gauge Performance

The weighing gauge registered 18.92 mm for the same 25 mm event, with a more accurate continuous record of rainfall rate. Evaporative losses of ~0.1 mm per hour during inter-event periods require baseline subtraction. The gauge requires periodic re-zeroing (monthly) but provides superior data for hydrologic modeling and storm intensity analysis.

## Recommendation and Installation Decision

The weighing gauge was selected for permanent installation due to its superior storm rate resolution. Maintenance is higher, but the continuous rainfall profile data supports research on erosion thresholds and infiltration dynamics.
