# Bird Survey Volunteer Training Notes

## Field Identification Fundamentals

New volunteers complete a 2-hour field session learning to identify target species by sight and sound. Emphasis is placed on separating look-alikes: Song Sparrow vs. Lincoln's Sparrow, and Willow Flycatcher vs. Least Flycatcher. Volunteers practice with field guides and recorded species vocalizations before the first formal survey. Binoculars (10×42 recommended) are provided for participants without optics.

## Survey Protocol and Timing

Surveys are conducted during the 05:00–09:00 window when bird vocalization rates peak. Volunteers learn to walk transects at a slow, steady pace (0.5 km/hr) while recording all birds seen or heard within 50 m of the path. The 50 m radius is practiced using marked distances in a parking lot before field deployment.

## Data Recording Skills

Volunteers are trained to record species, count, location, and behavior type on standardized forms. Common behaviors include feeding, singing (territorial), nesting activity, and fledgling dependence. Uncertain identifications are recorded with a "?" to allow for later review by experienced staff. Photographic aids showing field marks are provided for reference during surveys.
