# Camera Trap Deployment Notes

## Camera Height and Angle

Wildlife cameras are mounted at 1.2 m above ground level on trees or fence posts, angled 15–20 degrees downward toward the expected travel corridor. This height positions the camera lens in the mid-body zone for terrestrial mammals (deer, coyote, raccoon), maximizing species identification reliability. Cameras pointing level or upward tend to capture excessive sky and fail to record smaller species like foxes and mustelids.

## Trigger Sensitivity and Range

Motion sensors are configured with medium sensitivity to reduce false triggers from wind-blown vegetation. Effective detection range for animals 0.5 m tall is approximately 8 meters at perpendicular approach. Cameras are positioned to maximize side-on approaches rather than head-on or tail-on angles, which compromise body-size estimation and gait analysis.

## Site-Specific Placements

Cameras are deployed along established wildlife trails identified by tracking signs (scat, hoof prints, scratch marks). Trail confluences and water-crossing points receive priority for placement. Deployment sites are marked with flagging tape 50 m upwind to aid in relocation during maintenance, and GPS coordinates are recorded at the time of installation.
