# Middlemarch — Summary

## Book 1-2: Dorothea and Casaubon

Dorothea Brooke, a young, idealistic woman in a provincial English town, becomes engaged to the pedantic scholar Edward Casaubon, hoping to find intellectual partnership and spiritual purpose. Her sister Celia marries the gentleman farmer James Chettam. Dorothea's dreams of intellectual life with Casaubon quickly become disillusionment when she realizes his scholarship is sterile and his affections cold.

## Book 3-4: Lydgate Arrives

Tertius Lydgate, an idealistic young doctor, arrives in Middlemarch intent on medical reform and scientific advancement. He encounters Rosamond Vincy, beautiful and conventional, daughter of a prosperous manufacturer. Lydgate becomes infatuated, and though he initially resists, he gradually drifts into engagement, betraying his intellectual ambitions for domestic comfort.

## Book 5-6: Entanglements

Dorothea's marriage to Casaubon deteriorates; he dies, leaving a codicil forbidding her to marry his cousin Will Ladislaw. Will, a free spirit with artistic ambitions, has come to Middlemarch to assist Casaubon but begins falling in love with Dorothea. Lydgate's marriage to Rosamond becomes a trap—her extravagance and social ambitions undermine his medical ideals, and financial troubles accumulate.

## Book 7-8: Resolution and Compromise

Dorothea, freed from Casaubon's prohibition by Will's devotion, chooses love and marriage to Will over wealth and social status. Lydgate, compromised by debt and domestic discord, abandons his scientific ambitions for a respectable but conventional medical practice. The novel closes with each character finding a kind of peace—not through triumph of ideals but through accommodation to provincial reality and human limitation.
